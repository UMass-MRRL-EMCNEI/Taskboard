## Abstract

We designed nurse-focused robot manipulation training tasks tested by professional nurses as a resource for evaluating the capabilities and performance of robotic technology designed to assist nurses. To compare performance on these nurse-focused training tasks with current robotic small-parts manufacturing benchmarks, we conducted a usability experiment. Seven professional nurses completed two tasks and one workflow simulation using both their natural hands and robot data collection grippers. The two tasks included (1) the custom nursing task set designed for sterile procedure preparation, focused on surface preparation, drape manipulation, and sterile field item addition subtasks, and (2) the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Robotic Grasping and Manipulation Competition Task Board focused on threading, insertions, belt drive assembly, and wire routing subtasks. The simulated task served as a realistic version of the nursing task set that incorporates clinical workflow. We examined whether subtask success observed during benchtop tasks corresponded to task success during the simulated nursing task. All participants successfully completed all tasks when using their hands, establishing task feasibility. In contrast, participants had substantial difficulty using robot grippers. Threading operations on the NIST Task Board were universally unsuccessful, while the sterile drape placement task and the simulated nursing task were predominantly unsuccessful. The results suggest that specific bench-top manipulations capture dexterity limitations that also impede performance in realistic nursing procedures. These findings support developing nursing-focused robot benchmarking resources that help roboticists evaluate platform capabilities before conducting costly task-specific experiments.

## Background

### Motivation and Objectives
Using a nurse-identified task of interest—setting up a sterile field to assist a nurse performing a sterile procedure—this study engaged registered nurses to perform tasks using their natural hands and handheld data collection grippers (modified Universal Manipulation Interface [UMI] grippers) representing an assistive autonomous robot. The primary goal was creating nurse-focused manipulation training tasks to evaluate robotic capabilities in healthcare workflows, while secondarily comparing these against manufacturing assembly benchmarks (NIST RGMC Task Board).

### Study Conditions & Participants
The within-subjects study evaluated 7 professional registered nurses ($47.3 \pm 13.6$ years old, $\ge 2$ years experience) across two actuator conditions:
- **Concentrated Load Grippers (C):** Modified handheld UMI grippers with angled, stiff fingers that concentrate contact force at fingertips, equipped with GoPro HERO13 Black cameras for IMU/video and Tobii Pro 3 eye-tracking glasses.
- **Hands (H):** Natural hands wearing standard nitrile examination gloves where clinically appropriate.

## Experimental Setup & Tasks

### 1. Nursing Simulation (Realistic Workflow)
A low-fidelity clinical workflow simulation of intravenous (IV) dressing change preparation on a bedside table within a 3-minute limit:
- **Surface Preparation:** Dispensing and wiping down the table with Super Sani-Cloth germicidal wipes.
- **Sterile Drape Setup:** Aseptic opening, grasping by the border, and unfolding of a sterile drape.
- **Item Addition:** Picking and aseptic addition of 1 IV start kit, 3 Tegaderm dressings, and 3 gauze packages from a supply bin to the sterile field.

### 2. Nurse Task Set (Bench-top Proxies)
Isolated evaluations of sterile preparation using non-sterile generic proxies:
- **Surface Preparation:** Acrylic sheet covered in Glo Germ lotion divided into four segments.
- **Drape Manipulation:** Unfolding packaged drape proxies made from McKesson CSR wrap and autoclave pouches.
- **Item Addition:** Opening small proxy packages (cotton gauze in autoclave pouches).

### 3. NIST RGMC Manufacturing Task Board
Evaluated across 4 quadrants with 3 minutes allotted per quadrant:
- **Quadrant 1 (Threading):** Threading bolts into an aluminum plate and nuts onto bolts.
- **Quadrant 2 (Insertions):** Inserting shaped metal pegs, Ethernet cable snap-in, BNC connector twisting/locking, and gears.
- **Quadrant 3 (Belt Drive Assembly):** Placing a rubber belt, sliding the tensioner disc, and tightening with a hex key.
- **Quadrant 4 (Wire Routing):** Clicking wire ends into connectors and routing through retainers.

## Participant Evaluation & Perceived Similarity

Likert survey results (5-point scale: 1 = Not at all, 5 = Extremely well / Very large extent):

| Survey Question | Mean (SD) | Median | Mode |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1a. Clinical Similarity: NIST Task Board** | 2.14 (1.3) | 2 | 1 |
| **1b. Clinical Similarity: Nursing Task Set** | 4.71 (0.5) | 5 | 5 |
| **1c. Clinical Similarity: Nursing Simulation** | 4.71 (0.5) | 5 | 5 |
| **2. NIST captures nursing manipulation skills** | 2.71 (1.3) | 3 | 3 |
| **3. Nursing Task Set reveals unique limitations** | 3.71 (1.3) | 4 | 4 |

Friedman testing showed a significant difference across task similarity ratings ($\chi^2 = 10.8$, $p = 0.0045$). Post-hoc Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirmed that NIST differed significantly from both the Nursing Task Set ($p = 0.0310$) and Nursing Simulation ($p = 0.0345$), whereas the Nursing Task Set matched the realistic simulation in clinical fidelity ($p = 1.0$).

## Key Takeaways

- **Hardware Remains the Primary Bottleneck:** Despite recent advances in robot learning pipelines, end-effector kinematics and contact mechanics prevent execution of compliant clinical workflows.
- **Drape Manipulation is Highly Challenging:** Manipulating and unfolding thin, deformable sheets consumed the largest amount of time and produced the most timeouts.
- **Design Improvements Suggested by Nurses:** Participants indicated the need for compact gripper dimensions to operate inside cluttered spaces, as well as wider maximum aperture spans to grasp wider supplies like wipe canisters.
